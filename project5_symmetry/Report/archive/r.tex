\begin{abstract}
Predictive learning has been proposed as a general mechanism for forming
cognitive maps in the hippocampus: a recurrent neural network trained to
predict future observations from the statistics of an animal's movement
will develop place-cell-like spatial representations~\citep{levenstein2024}.
A natural question is how this framework behaves when the environment
provides ambiguous sensory information. We extend the Levenstein et al.
predictive RNN (pRNN) framework to ask: does rotational landmark symmetry cause map
formation to fail?

We train predictive RNNs in arenas with C1, C2, and C4 rotational
landmark symmetry and evaluate the resulting representations using four
metrics designed to detect the forms of failure that
standard spatial RSA cannot solve: Permutation-Aware Alignment (PAA),
Rotational Autocorrelation (RA), C2 Contrast, and a Symmetry Collapse
Index (SCI). We find that map formation does not fail under C4 symmetry.
Instead, a stable spatial map forms, but is geometrically distorted: units
develop rate maps that are similar at rotationally equivalent positions,
and decoding precision degrades monotonically with symmetry order
(RA: S1\,=\,0.097, S2\,=\,0.114, S4\,=\,0.217). Global orientational degeneracy is prevented by the head direction
(HD) input: PAA gain remains near zero for all conditions
(S4\,$<$\,0.05). Removing HD input causes map formation to collapse
(sRSA: 0.615 $\to$ 0.260), confirming
that HD is necessary for global stability. Through these studies we can say that symmetry does not cause the cognitive map formation to fail but instead deforms the geometry of the formed representation. 
\end{abstract}



\section*{Introduction}

Levenstein et al.~(2024) showed that a recurrent network trained on sequential predictive loss spontaneously develops spatially structured representations, but validated this exclusively in an L-shaped arena chosen to ensure every position has a unique visual fingerprint. However, Most rodent electrophysiology experiments use square arenas. Square arenas are rotationally symmetric. The condition Levenstein's validation depends on (global observational discriminability) does not hold there. We ask what the Levenstein framework actually does under these conditions. Not what the hippocampus does. What the model does. The answer has direct consequences for how the framework should be used, what its representations are grounded in, and what any extension to more naturalistic environments should expect to find. (The hippocampus is a different question, and we can only speculate about that based on the model's behavior.)

The head-direction (HD) system tracks global orientation through path integration while landmark anchoring corrects drift via visual cues~\cite{taube1990, knierim1995}, but symmetric landmarks force these two mechanisms into direct conflict: the same landmark view appears at multiple rotations, so the HD state and the landmark signal become discordant. The double-rotation literature documents that place cells increase their number of firing fields in symmetric arenas~\cite{muller1987, knierim1995}, with multi-field fraction scaling with symmetry order, and these multi-field patterns emerge spontaneously in the hippocampus without explicit instruction~\cite{leutgeb2005}.

This project asks whether landmark symmetry causes global map degeneracy (where different seeds learn incompatible global orientations), local representational folding (where symmetric positions collapse within a single run), or both. A standard metric (seed-to-seed RSA) cannot answer this question because row permutations preserve pairwise distances, making it blind to both global rotations and local folding. We introduce three metrics that separate identifiability from entanglement: PAA and RA for global vs local, C2 Contrast for group structure, and use them to test predictive networks trained on arenas with graded rotational symmetry. We show that head-direction input prevents global degeneracy while local precision degrades monotonically with symmetry order, and that this partial-folding pattern explains multi-field place cell emergence, predicts specific lesion deficits, and uncovers why sRSA fails under observation aliasing.

The theoretical link between predictive learning and spatial representation
comes from the successor representation (SR)~\citep{stachenfeld2017}. The
SR encodes each state as the discounted expected future occupancy of all
other states: $M(s,s') = \mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t
\mathbf{1}(s_t = s') \mid s_0 = s\right]$. Under a random policy in an
open environment, the SR matrix has a structure similar to a Gaussian
centered at each state, producing place-cell-like activity
profiles~\citep{stachenfeld2017}. Critically, the SR geometry is shaped
by the transition structure of the environment, not just its physical
geometry. This means that environments with rotational symmetry will have
SR matrices in which multiple distinct spatial positions are predicted to
share the same future state distribution: the SR entries for these
positions will be identical or nearly so. Whether a predictive RNN can
resolve this ambiguity, and if not, in what way its representation fails,
is onee of the central questions I want to address.




\section*{Methods}

The arena is an $18 \times 18$ MiniGrid (a grid-world environment where agents move along discrete rows and columns) with 324 positions. All conditions are matched on observational discriminability:
\[
\mathrm{ODI}=1-\frac{H(X\mid O)}{H(X)}=0.336.
\]
S4 has C4-equivalent observations under $90^{\circ}$ rotation (equivalence class size $H_2=4.0$), S2 has C2-equivalent observations under $180^{\circ}$ rotation ($H_2=2.0$), and S1 has no designed equivalence ($H_2=1.0$). The number of seeds is S4 $n=7$, S2 $n=5$, S1 $n=5$.

The model is a NormReLU pRNN (layer-normalized ReLU nonlinearity: $\mathrm{NormReLU}(\mathbf{x}) = \mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{x}) / \|\mathrm{ReLU}(\mathbf{x})\|_2$) with $N=500$ units. At time $t$, the hidden state updates as:
\[
h_t = \mathrm{NormReLU}(W_h h_{t-1} + W_o o_t + W_a a_t + W_d d_t),
\]
where $o_t$ is the egocentric observation, $a_t$ is the action, and $d_t$ is a 5-dimensional SpeedHD vector (speed + 4-way one-hot head direction). Training minimises multi-step prediction loss for $k=5$ steps:
\[
\mathcal{L} = \sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\hat{o}_{t+j} - o_{t+j}\|_2^2,
\]
over 80,000 training steps. A total of 17 seeds were run across all conditions (S4 $n=7$, S2 $n=5$, S1 $n=5$).

\noindent Three metrics address this blindness. PAA detects when rotated alignment between seeds beats identity alignment, revealing orientational divergence. RA measures whether units respond identically at rotationally equivalent positions, capturing unit-level aliasing. C2 Contrast tests whether the population code encodes group structure. PAA, RA, and C2 Contrast separate identifiability, entanglement, and population structure.

\section*{Why sRSA cannot detect the failure modes this project measures}

Seed-to-seed RSA correlates pairwise neural distances with pairwise spatial distances. However, this metric has a critical vulnerability: row permutations of the neural population vector preserve all pairwise distances, so rotated maps yield identical sRSA values even though they represent opposite global orientations.

Figure \ref{fig:srsa}Here we can see this more clearly: two fundamentally different failure modes (global rotation versus local folding) yield identical sRSA despite producing qualitatively different neural codes. This blindness matters empirically: sRSA is near-equal for S2 and S4 (~0.72 vs ~0.67), yet RA differs by nearly 2times (0.114 vs 0.217). A researcher relying on sRSA alone would conclude that C2 and C4 symmetry impose equivalent representational costs. They do not.

\section*{What I expect to find}

 Does global orientation remain stable across seeds? Does local precision stay high? This produces three mechanistically distinct failure modes: (1) global degeneracy and local collapse, (2) global stability but local folding (the case we observe), or (3) full robustness.

\section*{HD input prevents global orientational degeneracy}

Global map degeneracy did not occur. Cross-seed RSA alignment is S4 $0.977 \pm 0.002$, S2 $0.975 \pm 0.003$, S1 $0.974 \pm 0.001$. PAA gain is S4 $0.0015 \pm 0.0002$, S2 $0.0021 \pm 0.0004$, S1 $0.0000 \pm 0.0001$ with $p = 0.786$ and $r = -0.200$, indistinguishable across conditions and all below 0.05.

\section*{Unit-level aliasing scales monotonically with symmetry order}

Head-direction input cannot prevent local aliasing costs. Unit-level rotational autocorrelation (RA) was S4 0.217, S2 0.114, S1 0.097, scaling directly with symmetry group order (4-fold, 2-fold, 1-fold respectively). S4 versus S1 yields $p = 0.036$ and $r = -1.0$ (the smallest p-value n=5 and n=7 can produce; r hits its maximum because distributions are non-overlapping). Spatially tuned units (explained variance score, EVS $> 0.1$) comprise S4 1.8\%, S2 3.7\%, S1 4.7\% of the population.

\section*{Decoding degrades while population collapse stays partial}

Population compression stays graded despite unit-level aliasing. Symmetry Collapse Index (SCI) is S4 $0.889 \pm 0.002$, S2 $0.892 \pm 0.000$, S1 $0.984 \pm 0.002$; S4 versus S1 gives $p = 0.036$ and $r = -1.0$. Linear decoding error worsens: S4 $0.505 \pm 0.014$, S2 $0.448 \pm 0.014$, S1 $0.442 \pm 0.012$ ($p = 0.036$, $r = -1.0$). S4 compresses symmetry-related pairs by 11\% relative to random pairs. The spatial distribution of these errors is not uniform: decoding errors in S4 concentrate near the symmetry axes of the arena (Figure~\ref{fig:decode}), exactly where positions related by 90$^\circ$ rotation are hardest to distinguish. The dissociation between RA and SCI is notable: S4 and S2 show substantially different unit-level aliasing yet indistinguishable population compression ($p = 0.786$).

\section*{Distance geometry shifts toward path-dependence under symmetry}

Symmetry transforms distance geometry. The DTG (Distance-Topology Gap, $\Delta_{\text{TG}}$) is S4 $-0.052 \pm 0.003$, S2 $-0.011 \pm 0.003$, S1 $-0.020 \pm 0.004$ ($p = 0.036$, $r = 1.0$). All gaps are negative, indicating city-block dominance, but the gap widens in S4. Under $C_4$ symmetry, inter-quadrant Euclidean structure collapses because identical observations push those positions toward similar hidden states; path length within quadrants dominates the learned distance geometry.

\section*{C2 symmetry is encoded as group structure in the population code}

The population code encodes group structure, not all symmetries uniformly. A negative C2 Contrast (mean$_{\text{C2}}$ distance $<$ mean$_{\text{C4}}$ distance) indicates that positions related by 180\degree\ are more similar, consistent with C2 subgroup encoding. In S4, all quadrants are equally confusable, so inter-quadrant pairs compress identically and subgroup structure vanishes.

\section*{Head Direction Input is Necessary for Global Map Stability}
\label{sec:hdablation}

The PAA analysis in the symmetry sweep showed that HD input plays a
central role in preventing global orientational degeneracy. To test this
directly, we trained three variants of the pRNN in the S4 arena (the
condition with highest symmetry order, C\textsubscript{4}): HD\_FULL (the standard model), HD\_ABLATED (head
input zeroed: $\mathbf{d}_t[1{:}] = \mathbf{0}$), and HD\_DEGRADED (head set to
uniform: $\mathbf{d}_t[1{:}] = 0.25\cdot\mathbf{1}$). Speed was preserved in all
conditions.

Removing head input caused map formation to fail completely. Spatial
sRSA dropped from $\sim$0.615 in HD\_FULL to
$\sim$0.260 in HD\_ABLATED (Figure~\ref{fig:hdablation}),
well below the sRSA map formation threshold of $0.40$ used in~\citet{levenstein2024}. HD\_DEGRADED, which provides
head information but destroys its directional specificity by making all
four head channels equally active, produced the same failure
(sRSA\,$\approx$\,0.258). This shows that it is
specifically the directional content of the HD signal, not just its
presence, that matters for map formation.

The mechanism is visible in the training loss. The final loss for ablated
conditions ($\sim$0.0190) was approximately $8\times$ higher than
for HD\_FULL ($\sim$0.0025), indicating that the network
without head direction simply cannot predict future observations
accurately even after 80k training steps. Without knowing where the agent
is headed, the optimal strategy becomes to predict the mean observation:
which carries no spatial information. Mean field coherence confirmed this:
HD\_ABLATED networks had a coherence of $\sim$58.96
(highly disordered activity), compared to $\sim$13.64 for
HD\_FULL.

Intrinsic manifold dimensionality was also lower in ablated networks
(ID\,$\approx$\,5.34 vs 6.09 for
HD\_FULL), so the loss of spatial structure comes with
a genuine reduction in the representational degrees of freedom, not just
a degradation in spatial tuning. Together, these results confirm that HD
input is not a passive contextual signal but a necessary input for the
predictive learning objective to produce spatially organized representations.
This is consistent with the known role of head direction signals in
anchoring hippocampal spatial representations to a global reference
frame~\citep{lever2002}.

\section*{How the Levenstein architecture and my project relates to the hippocampus work}

This model provides a mechanistic account of the known scaling of multi-field place cell fraction with arena symmetry order~\cite{muller1987, knierim1995}. This follows directly from the RA result: existing hippocampal recording datasets from symmetric arenas can be stratified by symmetry order and the fraction of multi-field units measured. The RA result reproduces this scaling: multi-field fraction scales as $|C_4| > |C_2| > |C_1|$, matching the symmetry groups: RA = 0.217, 0.114, 0.097 respectively. This follows from the computational finding that high-RA units respond identically at rotationally equivalent positions and cannot disambiguate arena quadrant: precisely the dual-frequency structure of multi-field place cells recorded in symmetric arenas~\cite{muller1987, knierim1995}.

I do have some additional speculation about how this idea generalizes to complex abstract spaces but thats out of scope for this project.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Partial Folding as the Generic Response to Environmental Symmetry}

The central finding of this project is that the pRNN framework does not fail
under landmark symmetry. A network trained to
predict future observations in a C4-symmetric environment does not produce
a globally degenerate or random representation. Instead, it forms a map, but one that is geometrically compressed along the symmetry axes of
the environment. It is called partial folding: the network learns the true
spatial structure of the arena, but units develop activity patterns that
are correlated at rotationally equivalent positions, reducing the
precision of the spatial code without destroying it.

This is the right outcome looking at this problem from a computational standpoint. The task does
not require the network to distinguish between positions that are
genuinely indistinguishable from the agent's sensory perspective. Under
C4 landmark symmetry, positions separated by 90\degree~do produce the
same observations on average. The network's representational geometry
honestly reflects this ambiguity. The SR framework predicts exactly this
outcome: if two positions have similar expected future state distributions
(as they must when the arena looks the same from both), their SR entries
will be similar, and a network that learns the SR will represent them as
nearby in neural space~\citep{stachenfeld2017}. What we observe as partial
folding is the network faithfully encoding the transition structure of a
symmetric environment.

\subsection{The Role of head Direction}

What is the role of HD input in answering the following question? What is the difference between global orientational degeneracy (the
network cannot tell which way it is facing) and local representational
aliasing (the network represents multiple positions similarly). HD input
prevents the first but not the second. Without head direction, the
predictive objective becomes unachievable, and the network abandons the
attempt to form a spatial representation entirely. With head direction,
a stable global orientation is maintained (PAA gain $< 0.05$), but the
local folding induced by the landmark structure persists.

This dissociation maps naturally onto what is known about the biological
system. Head direction cells in the anterior thalamus and entorhinal cortex
provide a consistent allocentric orientation signal that is
necessary for anchoring hippocampal place fields to a global reference
frame~\citep{lever2002}. My results show that this anchoring function
is precisely what head input provides to the predictive RNN: it resolves
the ambiguity about global orientation, but cannot resolve the local
ambiguity created by observation aliasing. The remaining
aliasing reflects environmental structure rather than a failure of the
network.

\subsection{Representational Quality and the Spectral Gap}

The spectral analysis reveals a negative correlation between representational quality (measured by sRSA) and the spectral gap of the recurrent weight matrix. The spectral gap (the distance between the largest and second-largest eigenvalues) reflects the stability and "mixing time" of the recurrent dynamics. In symmetric environments, we find that the spectral gap narrows as sRSA increases. High-quality spatial maps in symmetric arenas do not emerge from "stronger" or more stable recurrent attractors, but rather from dynamics that are poised near a symmetry-breaking transition. This poise allows the network to maintain high-dimensional within-quadrant structure while tolerating the global ambiguity induced by the landmarks. The spectral gap thus serves as a dynamical signature of the "partial folding" we observe at the representational level. More work needs to be done here to understand what exactly is happening and is addressed in the future works section. 

\subsection{Limitations and Future Directions}

A couple of things to keep in mind. The HD ablation only used 2 seeds to test the idea. Same reason - trying out many configurations on the lab RTX 4070 meant I could only run a few seeds for each once things worked.

So here we've looked at what the representation looks like, but we haven't checked if downstream areas can actually use it. The folding we see might make it harder for them to read out position, or they might have ways to work around it. Running multi-environment experiments will tell us.
